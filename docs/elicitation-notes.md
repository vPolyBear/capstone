# Elicitation Interview Guide

## Before you sit down

- **Pick a real human** who has the problem. Not a classmate being polite. Not you.
- **Say the boundary out loud:** "I am not going to build everything you say. I am
  trying to understand what actually happens today." This buys you honest answers
  instead of a wish list.
- **Bring nothing to show.** A prototype turns an interview into a review. Later.
- **Record the date.** A requirement without a date is a rumor.

## The eight questions

Ask them in this order. The order matters: present tense before future tense,
behavior before opinion.

1. **"Walk me through the last time you did this. Start from the beginning."**
   You want the *episode*, not the summary. Interrupt only to ask "and then what?"

The last time I had serious stress was yesterday. F
I was in a forest and I have a phobia of insets and some crawled on me and I had a panic attack because of it and I could not think straight. F
Though I have struggled with stress everyday since I've been born because life is stressful and there is always a lot of anxiety and anxious thoughts going through my brain. F

2. **"What did you use to do it?"**
   A spreadsheet, a whiteboard, a group chat, a paper list, nothing. Whatever it is, that is your competition and your data model.

I used the environment I was in and took a walk and I went where there was less inscets, I then focused on how I was feeling and expressed what I was feeling to other people and that helped it go away. F
I also definitely use some breathing exercises to calm my nervous system down and I do things that I like if its things like going on a walk or petting my dog. F
I talk to who knows me and can handle me if I'm in a crisis mode. F

3. **"Where did that go wrong the last time?"**
   Failures are specific; satisfaction is vague. This question produces requirements.

The last time I was really in a panic mode, like I could not, like nothing I did really helped. F
It took a few hours to center myself and there was nothing really anyone could do about it but time really helped me. F

4. **"What did you do when it went wrong?"**
   The workaround is a feature request wearing a disguise.

I use methods that were given to me, that I've learn throughout life experiences. F
I use a lot of methods more on the social side. F
For me that works well. O
I talk to people about my feelings, write them down, and have people read it. F

5. **"How often does this happen? How long does it take?"**
   Numbers. Push for a number even if it is a guess, then write "estimated."

Throughout the day I get stressed multiple times, it could be up to 10 different times about different reasons and circumstances. F
It really depends on how exterme the stress is. It could last up to 4 to 5 hours or it could be like a minute. F

6. **"Who else touches this?"**
   You have just found a stakeholder you had not listed.

A lot of people around me are trying to help me get better and get less stressed or help me manage it. F
When they see that I'm in a hysterical state or see that I can't be calmed down, it makes the people around me sad because they can't help me and don't know what to do for me but when I get through it on my own that makes me proud that I got through it. F

7. **"If this problem disappeared tomorrow, what would change about your day?"**
   The answer is the rationale line for half your requirements.

If stress and anxiety was completely earsed from my life I think that it would impact me more negatively then just trying to manage it on my own as it helps build my character and how I deal with my emotions. O
I swear if it wasn't in my life I won't be as prepared to go throughout difference experiences in my life, like I won't be prepared to manage those as well. O

8. **"What is the part I have not asked about?"**
   Ask it. Then be quiet for a full ten seconds. The silence does the work.

One thing I would like to add is that stress comes in different forms for everybody and theres a difference between stress and anxiety. F
Certain methods normally work more with stress but if they don't then it's more so anxiety and its hard to tell on your own what you are feeling. F
I would strongly recommend getting help because you are not alone. O

## Questions to avoid, and why

| Do not ask | Why | Ask instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Would you use an app that…?" | Everyone says yes to a hypothetical. | "What do you do today?" |
| "Do you want feature X?" | You have handed them your design to rubber-stamp. | "Where does it go wrong?" |
| "How should this work?" | You are outsourcing the job you are being graded on. | "What has to be true for this to be worth opening?" |
| "Is this important?" | Every feature is important in the abstract. | "If you could only have one of these two, which?" |

## After the interview — same day, within an hour

- [X] Transcribe raw notes. Do not clean them up yet; keep the words they used.
- [X] Mark every sentence as **F** (a fact about today), **W** (a want), or **O** (an opinion).
      Facts become requirements first. Wants get triaged. Opinions get a rationale line.
- [X] Circle every noun they used more than twice. Those nouns are your data model.
    - stress, anxiety, people, feelings, manage, help, panic/crisis mode, methods
- [X] Write down the three things you assumed before the interview that are now wrong.
    - I thought that nobody really talked to others about their stress and I thought maybe that it was a one time thing but it seems that is one of the ways that helps her with her stress more often.
    - I didn't think it was even an option to write down you feelings then have someone read it.
    - I've always wished for my anxiety and stress to go away but I feel like she taught me that stress and anxiety has made me the way I am and made me, me. Where even though it sucks to have, that being able to manage it and figuring out how to manage it, is better than not having it because it has prepared me for a lot of other experiences.
- [X] Add one row to the Open Questions table for anything you could not answer.
- [X] Commit the notes with a dated message.

## The observation pass (do this too, if you can)

Twenty minutes of watching beats an hour of asking. Sit with them while they do
the task. Write down only what you see:

| It isn't possible (and doesn't feel ethical) for me to watch her while she has stress |

The "what surprised me" column is where the requirements nobody would have thought to ask for come from.
